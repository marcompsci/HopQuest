## Description
HopQuest is a cozy adventure where kids hop, drive, build and code their way to the castle, and learn something new at every step.

HOP ACROSS THE WORLD
Cross busy streets, rivers and lily pads with your buddy. Every crash is soft and silly: cars go BOING, water goes SPLISH, and you float right back with a safety tip. When the castle is done, new worlds open: Snowy Peaks, with icy lakes and winter science, then Sunny Shores, with surfboards, tide pools and a lighthouse, and Jungle Journey, with parrots, hippos and a temple at the top. Picture-book story scenes, read aloud in character voices, carry the adventure along.

LEARNING BUILT IN
Brain Gates ask quick questions about math, road safety, nature, science and words. Wrong answers just get a hint and another try. Questions match your child: picture counting for little learners, times tables and harder science for big learners, or let it adjust automatically.

HOP VILLAGE
Walk around a living village full of buddies to help. Seasons and weather change with the calendar, festivals come round all year, and kids can decorate a house of their own, grow a garden and pick a pet to walk with.

READING, MATH, SCIENCE AND MUSIC
Five reading games (letter sounds, spelling, sight words, rhymes and sentences), five money games (counting coins, paying, making change, comparing and adding prices), five guess-then-test science experiments, and a Music Maker with rainbow keys, drums and songs to learn.

CODING LAB
24 short lessons that teach real code a few blocks at a time:
• Web Builder: make a web page with HTML and CSS.
• Python Coder: variables, loops, if, functions and more.
• Game Maker: program your own little game.
Then try Free Build to make anything.

ROBOT LAB, CODE CLUB AND MACHINE WORKSHOP
Build a robot, then program it through nine Code Club puzzles: loops, "repeat until", and sensors that check what's ahead. In the Machine Workshop, solve puzzles with gears, levers and pulleys.

BUILD, RACE AND PLAY
• Fort Friends: build ramps, blocks and bridges to reach the fort, plus Builder's Island to build anything. No fighting.
• Bubble Kart: five cups with bubble shields, carrot boosts and jump ramps. Paint your kart. No attack items.
• Pillow Bop: a silly pillow-fight party in four arenas, from an Ice Rink to the Moon. Nobody gets hurt; fall off and a balloon floats you back.
• Sticker Book: collect 29 original buddies.
• My Castle: decorate your own room and share treats with your buddies.

PLAY TOGETHER
Two players on one device: race in Bubble Kart, bop in Pillow Bop, code as a team, and build forts together. Grown-up & Me mode lets a parent and child play side by side.

MADE FOR FAMILIES
• No ads, no in-app purchases, no chat, no accounts
• Nothing is ever sent to us: no analytics, no tracking
• Reads everything out loud for early readers
• Learning Report for grown-ups
• Optional daily play limit and stretch-break reminders
• No streaks, no daily-login rewards, no notifications
• Text Size, VoiceOver, Reduce Motion and left-handed controls supported
• Grown-up settings behind a parental gate, including optional play with nearby friends (off until a grown-up turns it on)

## App Review notes
HopQuest is a Kids Category app (ages 6–8). It has no ads, no in-app purchases, no third-party SDKs or analytics, no accounts of its own and no external links. All data (a first name or nickname, settings and progress) is stored only on the device.

Grown-up settings are behind a parental gate: tap the gear ("Grown-ups") at the top of the home screen and type the answer to the multiplication question shown (e.g. 7 × 8 = 56).

To see everything quickly: complete onboarding (any name). The home screen has PLAY (Hop Village), Coding Lab, My Castle, and tiles for Stickers, Fort Friends, Pillow Bop and Bubble Kart. "Adventure Map" opens the story levels, which unlock in order starting with Meadow Street; the Robot Lab stop leads to Code Club and the Machine Workshop once the robot is built, Snowy Peaks opens after Castle Hill and Sunny Shores after Peak Castle. "Together" has the two-player modes and "Stories" replays the story scenes. The Reading Nook and Math Market are menu cards and buildings in the village; the garden is below the house on the hill.

Play with others (new in 2.0): OFF by default. A grown-up can turn it on in Grown-up settings → Hop Village → "Play with others": Nearby only (Multipeer Connectivity, same room, no internet), or Game Center friends (only people already on the child's Game Center friends list; there is no matching with strangers). Players see each other's buddies walking in Hop Village and can play Brain Duels: five preset multiple-choice quiz questions. There is no chat, no free text, no voice and no way to send anything a child types. The only name shared is the buddy's name (e.g. "Pip"), never the child's name. Game Center follows the device's Screen Time multiplayer restrictions. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Coding Lab's Web Builder previews the page the child builds in a web view. It loads only that local HTML with JavaScript off, a content policy that blocks all network loads, every navigation cancelled and no address bar, so it cannot reach the internet.

The Local Network prompt appears only after a grown-up turns on "Nearby only".

No sign-in is required.

## App Privacy (nutrition label)
**Data Not Collected.** The developer receives nothing. When a grown-up turns on play with others, the device shares a random session key, the buddy's name and character, the buddy's position in the village and duel answers directly with the other players' devices (or through Apple's Game Center). None of it reaches the developer or any third party besides Apple, and none of it identifies the child.

## Export compliance
When uploading the build, App Store Connect asks about encryption. Answer that the app uses only the encryption built into iOS (Multipeer Connectivity and Game Center), which is exempt, so no documentation is needed. Please double-check this answer yourself: you're responsible for it.
